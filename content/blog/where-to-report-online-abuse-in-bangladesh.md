Most online abuse in Bangladesh is never reported to anyone. ActionAid found that **85 percent** of women who had experienced violence online never complained ([Prothom Alo, 27 November 2022](https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/p5uqt9qjl3)), and of everyone recorded as a victim of cyber violence in the first half of 2025, **97 percent were women and children** ([Prothom Alo, 9 July 2025](https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/7gdiyso4ho)).

Part of that is the well-founded belief that nothing will come of it. Part of it is simply not knowing where to go.

This is the practical version. What exists, what each route is actually for, and what to do first.

## Do this before anything else

Evidence is the thing most complaints lack, and it is the thing that disappears fastest. Before reporting, before replying, before blocking:

1. **Screenshot everything**, showing the content, the account name, and the surrounding thread. Not a crop of the words.
2. **Copy the link to the post or comment**, and separately the link to the account's profile.
3. **Note the date and time** you captured it.
4. **Do not edit the images.** No cropping out timestamps, no highlighting. An altered screenshot cannot be verified by anyone.
5. **Only then block or report** to the platform. Blocking can remove your own access to the evidence.

A deleted post is not recoverable by you, and usually not by the police either. Whatever you did not capture is gone.

## The numbers

| Who | Number | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| National emergency | **999** | Immediate physical danger. Police, fire, ambulance. Open all hours |
| Women and children helpline | **109** | Government helpline for violence against women and children. Free, all hours |
| Child helpline | **1098** | For anyone under 18 |
| Police Cyber Support for Women | **01320-000888** | Online harassment, blackmail, image abuse. Staffed for women complainants |
| National legal aid | **16430** | Free legal advice, office hours |
| Online complaint | [cybercrime.gov.bd](https://cybercrime.gov.bd) | Written report, if you would rather not call |

If there is any threat to your physical safety, 999 comes first and everything else can wait.

## What changed in 2026

In January the government approved two ordinances covering harassment, and the detail that matters here is the scope: the protections explicitly bring **sexual harassment through social media, email, messaging platforms and other digital technologies** within their purview, rather than leaving online conduct in a gap ([The Daily Star, 30 January 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/crime-justice/news/two-ordinances-protect-women-kids-all-spaces-4094071)).

The same reporting describes a requirement, following a writ petition, that every workplace and institution form a **five-member harassment complaint committee headed by a woman**, and that the guidelines are to be treated as law until legislation is made.

Two practical consequences:

- If the harassment came from a colleague or a classmate, there is supposed to be an internal committee with a woman at its head, and it is supposed to investigate. You can ask for it by name.
- Online conduct is no longer easily dismissed as outside the rules.

This is a change on paper. The Daily Star has documented the gaps between the legal framework and enforcement in practice ([17 December 2025](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/gaps-laws-enforcement-fuel-cyber-violence-against-women-4061176)), and nothing here should be read as a promise that a complaint will be acted on.

## What each route can actually do

Being honest about this saves people from the second disappointment.

**The police, including cyber support.** Can investigate, can require platforms to act in some circumstances, and can bring a prosecution. Slow, and dependent on the officer in front of you. Bring your evidence printed or on a device you can hand over, and keep your own copy.

**The platform.** Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and the rest can remove content and suspend accounts, which is often the fastest relief. They cannot give you any remedy beyond that, and removal destroys the evidence unless you captured it first.

**A workplace or institution committee.** Can discipline someone inside the institution. Cannot act on a stranger.

**Legal aid.** Can advise on whether what happened fits an offence, which is genuinely hard to judge from outside.

**Daag.** Keeps a public, verified record of what was written. It is not a complaint, it does not reach the police, and it produces no legal consequence. What it does is make the statement durable, searchable and attributable after the original is deleted.

Those are different tools. Using the archive is not a substitute for reporting to anyone, and reporting to the police does not preserve the public record.

## If you want it in the archive

[Submit it here](/submit). You will need the link to the commenter's profile, the link to the source post, and an unedited screenshot. Typing out the comment is optional and is what makes the entry findable later.

Nothing is published automatically. A moderator opens the links, checks the screenshot has not been altered, settles the category, and then approves, rejects, or asks for more, usually within three to five working days. You get a reference number to check the status.

Your email and your relationship to the incident are never published, and the person named cannot see who reported them. Anyone named can [appeal](/appeals), and a moderator other than the one who approved it reviews the evidence within fourteen working days.

More on how all of this works, including the questions people ask most often, is on the [about page](/about).

## One thing worth knowing

The comment you are looking at now will probably be deleted. That is the ordinary life cycle of this material: it is written in public, it does its work, and it is removed when the story stops trending or when its author thinks better of it.

Whether or not you report it anywhere, capture it now. Deciding later what to do with a screenshot is possible. Recovering a screenshot you never took is not.
