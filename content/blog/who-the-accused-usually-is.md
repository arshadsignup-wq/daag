Advice about keeping girls safe in Bangladesh tends to concentrate on strangers, darkness and distance. Do not go out late. Do not travel alone. Do not trust people you do not know.

Read this year's convictions together and that advice is aimed at the wrong place.

## The 2026 verdicts, side by side

| Verdict | Who the accused was | Reported |
|---|---|---|
| Life term for raping a 15-year-old student in Jamalpur | **An imam** of a mosque, who taught her | [26 Oct 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/crime-justice/news/imam-gets-life-term-over-rape-girl-4307536) |
| Death sentence for raping a 13-year-old in Dhaka | **Her stepfather** | [29 Sep 2026](https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/crime/7abjonw82b) |
| Life until death for raping a child in Chattogram | **A neighbour** | [Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/district/jsgxcm9x4y) |
| Life term for raping a 15-year-old in Gopalganj | **A man known to her**, who threatened her | [15 Oct 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/crime-justice/news/man-gets-life-sentence-raping-15-year-old-girl-gopalganj-4299456) |
| Death and life terms over a gang rape at the MC College hostel in Sylhet | **Men at the institution** she was visiting | [14 Jul 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/crime-justice/news/one-sentenced-death-three-life-term-impsonement-gang-rape-mc-college-4223566) |

In the Jamalpur case the prosecution described offences committed in late 2020 after the accused lured the girl with a promise of marriage. In Gopalganj, the case statement describes a man who took the girl to an isolated spot after threatening her.

The pattern is not subtle. An imam, a stepfather, a neighbour, a man known to the victim, men inside an institution. In most of these the accused had access because he was trusted, or because trust was not required: he was simply already there.

## The exceptions prove the shape

Two of the year's most reported cases do involve someone the victim did not know.

The [Cumilla case](/blog/what-a-confession-does-not-settle) involved an auto-rickshaw driver the girl hired. The [Tangail case](/blog/180-days-in-law-three-years-in-practice), in which three men received life terms, involved a woman travelling on a bus.

These are real and they are serious. They are also the cases that get the most coverage, which is part of why public understanding is skewed towards them. A stranger attacking a woman in transit is a story with a clear villain and an obvious lesson. A stepfather is a story with no lesson that anyone wants to hear.

## Why the misdirection is costly

If the danger is imagined as external, then safety is imagined as restriction. Stay in, come home early, do not travel alone, do not go to that place.

Every one of those measures is useless against an imam who teaches you, a stepfather in your house, or a neighbour on your lane. Worse, each of them increases the time a girl spends in the settings where the convictions above actually happened.

It also shapes what happens after. When the accused is known to the family, reporting means accusing someone the household depends on, lives beside, or answers to. That is a large part of why cases take as long as they do, and why so many never start. The [three years and seven months](/blog/180-days-in-law-three-years-in-practice) a case takes on average is not only a court backlog; it is also the time it takes for a family to decide, and to keep deciding, to continue.

## What the comments do with it

This is where Daag's record begins.

When the accused is a stranger, the comment section is mostly about punishment. When the accused is known to the victim, something different happens: the family comes under examination. Why did the mother not know, why was the child left there, why did they not move, what kind of household is this.

The archive contains both responses. The second is more corrosive, because it arrives when the family is already deciding whether pursuing the case is survivable.

## A caution about this reading

These are five reported verdicts, not a dataset. They were selected because they were reported in 2026 and the relationship between accused and victim was stated in the reporting; the full picture would require court records across all ninety-nine tribunals rather than the cases that reached a newspaper.

What can be said honestly is narrower than a statistic and still worth saying: in the convictions reported this year where the relationship is known, the accused was overwhelmingly someone the victim already knew. Any safety advice built on the opposite assumption is pointed away from where the evidence is.
